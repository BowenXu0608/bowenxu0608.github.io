# 2026-11-02 Engineering log

## Objective explorer handbook, and half the area with ten huts, with one explorer
<!-- project-update-log:2026-11-02:objective-handbook-ten-huts -->

- Reason：The user asked for the explorers' handbook to be objective, with no strategy, then for a world of half the area with 10 supply points in every district, checked with one explorer on DeepSeek.
- Implementation：PolarNpcManual states rules and facts only: every piece of advice (eat early, reach shelter before night, chains of walks, move on while fed, the cold-walk tent rule, put stoves out, carry the tent and others) is removed, and neutral facts replace what the rules need. New trial switch PolarCompactWorld.Huts keeps that many of v19's 10 huts in each district but Pole Station's (Tools/PolarWorldCheck --huts, PolarNpcDayDiagnostic -npcDiagnosticHuts). Trial worlds keep the cracks that find room. The diagnostic resets the trial switches only after Play Mode ends, so the closing save matches the trial world.
- Validation：Shared rules 2748 and CloudNpcCheck 204 pass. Seed 777 with AreaShare .5 and Huts 10: 206 sites, the route proofs and save round trip pass, and only the area, walk-relief and pond-size calibration checks fail (as with 70% area). Grant alone (st-a50h10b, $0.13) stayed home five days with Pole Station as his goal, then walked starving to the 421 m hut (4 tins) and died of hunger after 7.5 days, having searched 2 of his 10 huts.
- Decisions and scope：The handbook change is kept as the user asked. The 10-hut world stays a trial switch; nothing changes the shipped world or its stock.
- Remaining work：One run cannot separate the handbook change from the world change. The same total food over more huts leaves a district hut a mean 27 food units instead of 40, and the user's request that huts hold resources at the same level is not yet applied. The policy text before the handbook still asks explorers to take what a journey needs before setting out.
- Visual evidence:The handbook is the explorers' model prompt and the trial switches are off by default and outside the world version; the shipped world, its hash and every screen are unchanged, so no capture applies.

## Faces drawn as skin
<!-- project-update-log:2026-11-02:face-skin -->

- Reason：The user asked for visual improvements that could start at once and be confirmed; faces in the talk close-up read as flat paint, with a bright painted beard between the hair cards.
- Implementation：
  - Tools/polar_face_maps.py writes a normal map (luminance high-passed at 5 px), a mask map (smoothness by region: skin .40, lips .48, hair .22, eyes .88), a shared subsurface mask and, for the nine bearded people, the face with the beard under the cards darkened to 45%.
  - PolarFaceSkinAuthor binds them on the 18 PersonNFace materials as HDRP Lit subsurface scattering with the HDRP Skin diffusion profile; faces and maps import at 512 px, high-quality compression. The survivors (Frost, Moss, Shaw) keep their body texture.
  - PolarArtCapture gains an opt-in faces section: each person from 0.7 m in front of the eye, as in the talk close-up.
- Validation：
  - 8 close-up pairs (old face assets temporarily restored for vp-face-before, new in vp-face2) change 0.9-2.3% of pixels, on faces and beards; 8 portrait pairs (vp-before, vp-final2).
  - Package 0.6.92-test (vp-01): gameplay 387, weather 80 and ui 463 pass (Logs/PolarV7/0.6.92-test/vp-01).
- Decisions and scope：Faces stay 512 px; the survivors are outside this pass.
- Remaining work：The subsurface pass's cost while a face is on screen was not measured separately.

## Snow without a repeating grid at distance
<!-- project-update-log:2026-11-02:snow-distance-tiling -->

- Reason：Open snow from about 20 m showed a grid of converging lines and dashes, plainest at night: repeats of the tiled ground maps and the FreshPowder maps' regular grain, which the broad sample magnified into a 1.7 m dimple grid.
- Implementation：PolarData.hlsl PolarGroundSurface: each tiled albedo's variation and the packed normal fade to the map's mean (level 12) as a tile shrinks from 64 to 16 px; the FreshPowder normal and the gloss read from its albedo fade out from 5 to 2 px a grain cycle; the broad sample reads no finer than level 8.5. The PolarGround twin includes the file.
- Validation：
  - Strongest periodic peak over its band's median (6-60 px) in the grid area: night 4.2 to 2.9, medium snow by day 5.7 and 5.4 to 3.4 and 3.3 (plain snow about 3); other b5-land, b3 and dist frames change at most 0.66% of pixels.
  - Package 0.6.92-test (vp-01): gameplay 387, weather 80 and ui 463 pass.
- Decisions and scope：Clear-day cirrus on HDRP's cloud layer was tried and left out: its forward-lit cirrus fed the sky light (up to a quarter more on the snow, nearly double at 07:00 at full weight) and lowered the sun's share the user chose on October 24; measurements in Logs/ProjectUpdateCapture/polar-art/vp-share and vp-share2.
- Remaining work：Two more texture reads a ground pixel, not measured separately. Cirrus would need a view-only layer kept out of HDRP's sky probes.

## Clear-day cirrus drawn only in the visible sky
<!-- project-update-log:2026-11-02:view-only-cirrus -->

- Reason：The user asked to try clear-day cirrus again as a layer that does not light the snow (HDRP's cloud layer had lowered the sun's share of the snow's light in 0.6.92-test).
- Implementation：
  - PolarCirrus.shader: a full-screen custom pass at AfterOpaqueAndSky (after the sky, before fog and clouds), so HDRP's ambient probe and sky reflections never see it; cirrus coloured from the sky behind them (85% whitened to the sun's colour, x2.2), opacity map x3.5, weighted by look and clear visibility (PolarHdrpPresentation).
  - Cirrus map: the cirrus channel of HDRP's DefaultCloudMap, one uncompressed 2048x1024 channel (Tools/polar_cirrus_map.py); PolarHdrpLensPass copies the camera colour for it; the HDRP build checks the shader.
- Validation：
  - Snow luminance (lower 40%, linear) with the pass on, off, on in one b2-hold session: 07:00 .3924/.3918/.3916, 14:00 .5704/.5700/.5698, within noise (the cloud layer had added 26-93%); the sky 12-15% brighter.
  - 10 clear-day pairs change 6-40% of pixels; medium snow and a clear night change no pixel by more than 8/255.
  - Package 0.6.93-test (ci-01): gameplay 387, weather 80 and ui 463 pass; gameplay peak 6.90 GB (6.64 GB on vp-01).
- Decisions and scope：Placed after the fog (BeforePreRefraction) the cirrus read harsher and lay over the volumetric clouds; the before-fog placement was kept.
- Remaining work：The pass's GPU cost (a full-screen copy and pass by clear day) was not measured separately.

## Better weather and the ship from day 12; what everyone knows at the start
<!-- project-update-log:2026-11-02:day12-start-knowledge -->

- Reason：The user asked for better weather from day 12, the radio on the first clear day from day 12, and for people to know Pole Station's place without thinking to go there, since in Acts I and II nobody knows they can be rescued (GDD 2.2, 3.3).
- Implementation：Weather: PolarWeather.Better steps from 0 to 1 at day 12 (period 66), no ramp. Ship: PolarRescue.FirstDay 12. Knowledge: PolarNpcManual drops the forecast and Pole Station's 'base and assembly point' and 'stocked beyond any hut'; the explorer's view gives Pole Station's distance, bearing and hours only; the rescue command and its words reach an explorer only after hearing the broadcast (HeardSchema); two handbook captions no longer give the weather's turn or a radio rescue point, and a quick-bar caption over the ten-word limit since 0.6.90-test was shortened. Package 0.6.94-test, build kd-02.
- Validation：Shared rules 2748/2748 and CloudNpcCheck 204 pass. Player modes on kd-01: gameplay 387, weather 80, ui 463 pass; handbook-revision failed on the 13-word quick-bar caption and passes 619 on kd-02, which differs only in that caption (Logs/PolarV7/0.6.94-test).
- Decisions and scope：Step the weather at day 12 rather than ramp; hide rescue from the shared instructions and schema rather than rename the command; keep Pole Station on everyone's chart as GDD 2.2 says.
- Remaining work：No live explorer run was made to see how explorers now start. Hut stock is still uneven (offered after the ten-hut trial, unanswered).
- Visual evidence:Only the weather schedule, the message's first day and what explorers are told change; each weather and the radio scene draw as before. ## Thin ice: ice from every side, the underwater view, the dip and the hands
<!-- project-update-log:2026-11-02:underwater-entry -->

- Reason：M2 of the approved deep-water plan: going through thin ice should show the ice from every side and a moment under the water, and the person should climb out onto ice that holds to its visible edge.
- Implementation：One hole line (PolarThinIce.Hole) for the art, rim colliders, explorers' footing and climbing out, with a 0.15 m capsule over broken plates; ice undersides with sky light through them; HDRP's underwater view in the active pond with the sea on its own layer; a 0.35 m dip of eye and body for about a second on going in; the hands drawn in the world in a pond; a lens film and a low-pass under water; the thin-ice session's darker, patchier slush and dished caps at loaded plates.
- Validation：Package 0.6.100-test (dm-02): gameplay 389, physical 3959, continue 36, performance 61, weather 80 and ui 463 pass; seed 777 passes 1103 world checks with its v23 hash unchanged; capture b4-deep-water passes 56 shots with no exceptions (dw-m2c).
- Decisions and scope：Thin-edge transmission and a wet band on piece tops were left out: the captures read as ice without them and both would add a material variant and a diffusion profile to HDRP's global settings. The drawn body follows the dip; without it the eye sank into the treading body.
- Remaining work：A dark line where a rim strip laps the shore's snow, older than M2, goes to the shore work; M3 (the breakthrough) and M4 (release checks).

## Thin ice: the continuous breakthrough
<!-- project-update-log:2026-11-02:continuous-breakthrough -->

- Reason：M3 of the approved deep-water plan: the fall through thin ice should be one continuous event, the ice cracking from under the feet, its pieces folding into the water from where it broke, the water answering with rings, spray and bubbles.
- Implementation：Visual events from real transitions (ThinIceVisualEvent); the break point kept with a broken plate in saves (PolarIceSnapshot.struck, hitX, hitZ); cracks drawn as the exact edges of the plate's Voronoi network from its seeds (_PolarCrackMap), revealed outward from the load with stress; pieces folding in from the break point over 0.2-0.5 s; rings on the pond's HDRP water through one water decal (PondRings.mat, PolarIceRipples.shader, supportWaterDecals on); pooled spray, bubbles and foam (PolarThinIceEffects); pieces bobbing and shoved by a swimmer; a large piece's landing heard and explorers' gasp on coming up.
- Validation：Package 0.6.101-test (dm-03): gameplay 390, physical 3959, continue 36, performance 61, weather 80 and ui 463 pass; capture b4-deep-water passes 57 shots with no exceptions (dw-m3d).
- Decisions and scope：The drafted 128 px crack line map dashed near junctions and, widened, overran them, so the shader draws the exact bisector of the two nearest seeds instead. The 24-piece rigid-body simulation was not built: the decal rings move the water's normals only, so pieces have no height to ride, and the swimmer's shove stands in for it. The walk's load frames change nothing at the eye, since the cracks start under the feet; a close view from 2.4 m up (after only) shows them.
- Remaining work：M4 (performance gates, a load/unload soak, release checks).
