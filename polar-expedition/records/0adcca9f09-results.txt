# Terrain drawn near to far: the benchmark pond's GPU cost (0.6.154-test)

November 14, 2026. Package `Builds/HdrpMilestones/to-01`. RTX 4070 Laptop GPU, 2560 by 1440, High, TAA, Direct3D 11.

## In short

- The extra GPU time of the benchmark's pond part is not the pond. From the bank the camera looks west across the island, and
  nearly all of the island's distant terrain enters the view: 645 of its 672 blocks (8.8 million triangles), against 131 blocks
  (3.5 million) on the open-snow walk. From the open-snow spot, looking west costs the same extra time.
- 2.08 ms of the 2.73 ms pond-minus-snow gap is the GBuffer pass. About half of that is geometry (vertex work on triangles
  that end up hidden or smaller than a pixel) and up to 1.0 ms is ground shaded and then hidden by nearer ground.
- **Applied:** terrain is drawn near to far, after everything else (14 lines in `PolarWorldStreamer.cs`). The picture does not
  change. On the release benchmark the pond's GPU time falls by about 0.3 to 0.5 ms and the coast's by about 0.35 ms; open
  snow, the hut and the blizzard are unchanged.
- **Not reached:** the pond's P95 is 17.03 and 17.06 ms on the new package, still over 16.67 ms (17.13 and 17.42 before).
- **For a decision:** the larger part of the gap is the number of distant-terrain triangles in view. Lowering it changes how far
  ridges are drawn, so it was measured, not changed.

## What was measured

All diagnostics ran in a private copy of the Development Player `hb-03-dev` with its game assembly recompiled (`tools/`): a
probe that records the GPU time of every HDRP pass marker each frame (`PolarPassProbe.cs`), and a lab that holds fixed views
and switches one thing at a time (`PondLab.cs`). Host load was read once a second beside every run (`data/host-load.txt`).

### 2. It is the direction, not the pond (host quiet)

The same spot looking eight ways round, 90 frames each ([paired tables](data/lab-02-paired.txt)):

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Open-snow spot, looking east | 1.50 | 12.27 | 3.1 million |

| Open-snow spot, looking west | 4.26 | 15.89 | 12.1 million |
| Pond bank, looking south-east | 1.83 | 13.74 | 3.9 million |

What is in view ([pond](data/lab-02-census-pond.csv), [open snow](data/lab-02-census-snow.csv)): the world holds 672 distant
terrain blocks, 10.3 million triangles. At the pond 645 are in view; on the walk 131. The pond's own 55 plates are 9,018
triangles. Switched off in turn at the pond view, the refined blocks and the near window save 2.10 ms of GBuffer and the coarse
blocks 0.87 ms ([captures](data/lab-02-pond-groups-off.jpg)). The "everything else" group is not usable: switching it off
turned the scene to night.

### 3. Two candidate fixes at the fixed views (host quiet, four pairs each)

| Switch | Open snow, whole GPU | Pond, whole GPU | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Full depth prepass | +0.85 ms | +1.04 ms | GBuffer falls 0.11 and 1.01 ms, but the prepass costs 0.92 and 2.04 ms: a net loss |
| Every renderer near first | +0.20 ms | +0.24 ms | A loss |
| Every renderer far first | +1.44 ms | +1.25 ms | Shows the order matters |
| Terrain near first | -0.03 ms | -0.44 ms | The candidate |

HDRP leaves front-to-back sorting out of its opaque order (`HDUtils.k_OpaqueSortingCriteria`) and the game has no full depth
prepass, so the order terrain is drawn in decides how much hidden ground is shaded. The prepass's own cost, 2.04 ms for 10.2
million triangles with the simplest pixel shader, sizes the geometry cost: about 0.2 ms a million triangles in view.

## What changed

`Assets/Scripts/Polar/PolarWorldStreamer.cs`: each terrain renderer's sorting order is 1 plus its metres from the player, set
when the chunk is made and again once the player has moved 8 m (`Rank`, `OrderTerrain`). Opaque draws sort by sorting order
before material, so terrain is drawn near to far; everything else keeps order 0 and is drawn before it, so a hut's walls still
hide the ground behind them. `PolarPackage.cs` and `ProjectSettings.asset` carry the label 0.6.154-test. Nothing else.

## What the player sees

No change. At five views with the clock stopped (open snow, the same spot looking west, the pond, the hut at night, the coast)
the change off, on and off again: the share of pixels that differ by more than 8 levels between off and on is 0.28, 0.02,
0.02, 0.06 and 0.22%, and between the two captures with it off 0.17, 0.02, 0.02, 0.10 and 0.15%. The registered pairs are the
`*-before.png`, `*-after.png` and `*-comparison.png` files here. They come from the private Development Player with the change
switched off and on in one session, not from two packages.

## What remains

- The pond's P95 is still over 16.67 ms by about 0.4 ms, and the hut at night is over it too.
- Geometry: looking inland, about 2 ms of the GBuffer is vertex work on distant terrain. The blocks' resolution rings (256
  within 6 km of the player, 128 within 16 km) date from larger worlds, and today's island lies almost wholly inside the first.
  Fewer triangles there means coarser distant ridges, which is a visible trade-off: not changed, and its options not yet
  measured.
- Screen-space global illumination is 4.3 to 4.7 ms in every outdoor view, the largest single pass.
- About 0.5 ms of GBuffer at the pond beyond the same heading on open snow (4.80 against 4.26 ms) is not attributed; the
  earlier cap test accounts for 0.19 ms of it.
- The release gain at the pond (0.3 to 0.5 ms) is smaller than the Development Player's paired 0.85 ms. That run had background
  load and a slightly fuller variant; the quiet fixed-view test of the terrain-only order read 0.44 ms.

## Not run

The Player suite (gameplay, performance and the other modes) was not run on `to-01`, at the user's request for speed. The change
alters only the order of opaque terrain draws.

## Reproduction

`tools/make_rsp.py` writes the compile response file from Unity's own, `tools/run.py <run-id> [-polarPondLab <stages>]` runs
the private Player with the probe, `tools/analyze.py` and `tools/labstats.py` write the tables, `tools/bench.py <stage> <run-id>`
runs the release benchmark and removes the entry it adds to the player's history, and `tools/diffguard.py` stops the apps'
merge-base git diffs while runs are timed.
