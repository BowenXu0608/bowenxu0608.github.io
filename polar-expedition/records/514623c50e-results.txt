# Distant terrain detail rings halved; the loading page held over the first frames of play (0.6.157-test, `dr-01`)

November 14, 2026. The two leftovers of the frame-budget work that the user chose to fix ([final report](../frame-budget/final-report.md)):
1. the hut route's two steps over 16.67 ms, by halving the distance at which distant terrain drops detail, on condition of
   comparison pictures showing little influence in every situation;
2. the two slow frames as an expedition appears, by keeping the loading page up over them.

## 1. Distant terrain detail

### What changed

- `PolarStreamingPolicy`: a distant block has resolution 256 within 3 km of the focus, 128 within 8 km and 64 within 14 km (they
  were 6, 16 and 28 km, set for larger worlds).
- Loading still fills blocks within 6 km at 128 before play (`PolarStreamingPolicy.LoadingMeters`, `PolarWorldStreamer.LoadingResolution`),
  so an expedition's first picture is as it was; a block between 3 and 6 km keeps that 128.
- Nothing else: the near window (the 5 by 5 chunks round the player), collision, rules, saves and world hashes are untouched.

### The comparison: how it was made

One game session for each group of views, the clock stopped, the interface hidden, 2560 by 1440, High, seed 777. At each
stand the distant terrain was built with today's rings and captured, rebuilt with the halved rings and captured again from the
same camera, so the only thing that differs between the two pictures is the terrain's detail (`tools/RingsLab.cs`, a private
copy of the release Player; `tools/rings_compare.py`). Blocks took exactly the resolution a fresh load refines them to, which
is the least detail a player sees with either setting. At two stands today's setting was captured a second time after the
halved one: the difference between those two is the capture's own noise (snow glints, the stove's flicker).

27 views: five stands outdoors (open snow by the home hut, the highest ground within 2.5 km, 2 km inland, a pond's bank, the
coast), the compass directions at each, clear day, low sun (20:50), dawn (07:15), clear night, medium snow, and the home hut's
room at night.

### The comparison: result

![Share of pixels that differ, every view](rings/summary.png)

  of a level in every view.
- **Two captures of today's setting:** 0.001% to 0.16% of pixels, mean 0.2 to 0.4 of a level.
- **Where the difference is:** the fine snow-on-rock speckle of far rock faces and cliff bands, pixel by pixel. Ridge lines,
  the shapes of hills, tones and shadows are the same; no view shows a change of shape.
- **Indoors** (0.28% and 0.31%) the terrain is not in view at all: that difference is the stove lamp's flicker on the planks.

- **The pond's margin grows** from 0.5 to between 0.9 and 1.6 ms, and the hut route as a whole falls from 16.61 to 15.9 ms.
- **Go out** is under the budget in both runs. **Look round** is under it in one (16.55) and 0.06 ms over in the other
  (16.73), against 16.92 before; its share of frames over the budget falls from 11.3% to 3.9 to 5.6%. It is at the edge, not
  clear of it.
- **These runs are pessimistic.** The machine was in use: other programs took about 2 CPU cores in every run today (0.7 in
  0.6.155-test's), the WMI service alone 0.9 of one whenever the game ran, and the window keeper had to raise the game's
  window up to 25 times a run. In the first route run Windows' own maintenance (Disk Cleanup, Defender) left 0.3 GB of memory
  free at one point; steps the change does not touch read 0.3 to 0.5 ms worse there than on `fb-02`.

## 2. The loading page over the first frames of play

- **What changed.** `PolarMvpController.Entering` is true for the frame loading ends in and the two after it, and
  `PolarMvpUi.PageKey` keeps the loading page while it is. The play loop runs for the first time in those frames and its code
  is compiled then; that now happens behind the loading page, and the world appears on a frame of ordinary length.
- **Measured** (`python Tools/polar_save_rate.py --seconds 12 --first-frames`, release, no model; `data/first-frames-*.json`):

| Frame after loading ends | Before: page, length | After: page, length |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (loading's last) | world, 608 ms | loading, 997 ms |
| 1 | world, 74 ms | loading, 80 ms |
| 2 | world, 101 ms | loading, 77 ms |
| 3 | world, 25 ms | world, 19 ms |
| 4 and on | world, 8 to 17 ms | world, 6 to 20 ms |

- **Pictures** (`entry/first-frames-before-after.jpg`, from a second run in which saving each picture adds about 100 ms to a
  frame, so the page's 0.12 s fade spans fewer frames than in play; the lengths printed on it are the timing run's):

![The first six frames, before and after](entry/first-frames-before-after.jpg)

- The slow frames themselves are as long as before: the code is still compiled once. The loading page is on screen about
  0.15 s longer.

## Reproduce

```
python Tools/polar_play_routes.py --stage dr-01 --run-id <new id> --passes 3 [--only B]
python Tools/polar_save_rate.py --stage dr-01 --run-id <new id> --seconds 12 --first-frames [--first-shots]
python tools/patch_lab4.py; python tools/make_rsp2.py 1900b0aP.dag src4 build4   (the lab's assembly, then a copy of a release Player)
python tools/rings_lab.py <player copy> <run id>; python tools/rings_compare.py <run folder> <out folder>
```

`tools/patch_lab4.py` matches the policy's lines as they were before this change (6, 16 and 28 km); on this source its two
policy edits must name the new lines, and the lab's factor 1 is then the halved setting and 2 the old one.
