## Supply-walk trial switches: November 1, 2026

The user asked whether shorter supply walks would let explorers reach supplies; the explorer runs are in [polar-cloud-npcs](polar-cloud-npcs.md). Three switches make trial worlds without a new world version (`PolarCompactWorld.AreaShare` and `HutPull`, both 1 and off by default, and `Huts`, 0 and off). A world made with any of them has its own hash.
- **AreaShare** keeps that share of v20's area. The scale (`LinearFor`, `SelectHalf`), the macro land (`MacroFor`) and the least supply walk (`PolarWorldSettings.Validate`) take its square root, as v20 halved v19's.
- **HutPull** (`Pull`, in `GenerateCompact` for districts) moves every hut but the home hut that share of the way toward it on the template, nearest first, so every distance between two of them is that share.
  - Each takes the cell nearest its aim, searched outward up to 60 cells, that meets four tests: its 9x9 footprint is walkable inside the border; its door cells are reachable from the home hut's door on the template; it is at least 54 x share - 2 cells from every hut placed; and it is clear of gap boundaries by 24 cells.
  - Old footprints open, new ones close and rock obstacles are applied again; the spacing floor scales by the share.
  - A pulled hut whose door the later landforms cut off ("Detached critical hut entrance") is placed again at least 12 cells from there (`PullAvoid`, up to 12 tries). The list lasts the process, so a world read back without the navigation proof matches.
- **Huts**, when above 0, keeps that many of v19's 10 huts in each district except Pole Station's, instead of 7 (`HutsFor`, so `SelectHalf` thins to it and the site count follows: 206 sites at 10). It cannot add huts beyond v19's 10.
- In a trial world a district keeps the cracks that found room instead of failing (`AddCracks`): in the game's AreaShare .5, 10-hut world for seed 777 the full count did not fit round one district's huts and doors.
- With AreaShare or HutPull on, a district may hold 11 ponds, never more than the settings' own minimum (`PolarThinIce.ValidateWorld`; the library's reference world keeps its own).
- **Tools.** `Tools/PolarWorldCheck --compact --area <share> --hut-pull <share> --huts <n>`, and `PolarNpcDayDiagnostic -npcDiagnosticArea`, `-npcDiagnosticHutPull` and `-npcDiagnosticHuts`.

## Water is never solid; route flags: October 22, 2026 (0.6.40-test)

The user's playtest of 0.6.39-test found the player standing on the sea, unable to move (a copy of their save stood on the coast's shelf, 0.97 m under the sea plane).
- **Cause.** The sea was never water: `PolarSurveyPlayer.Move` set `inWater` only for thin ice and leads, the coast's last 70 rows blend to -8, 1 m under the plane at -7, and the step rule tested only the target, so a body over a refused cell (a bay floor, past z = 1024) was refused in every direction.
- **One water query.** `PolarWorldController.WaterLevel(position, out ground)` returns the level of a lead (at least sea level), a coastal bay, or the sea over the shelf (ground below sea level in the coast's last 70 rows only: inland ground may lie below the sea's datum, and package fx-01 counted a home hut there as sea) and up to `SeaEdge` (64 rows past the coast), NaN elsewhere, with the land's height; `Open` is ground or water. The player swims in any of them (cold-water rules unchanged), `FindLeadClimb` climbs out onto any bank at most 10 m above the water, `PlaceAt` and Continue accept water, and a step is refused only into a place that is neither, and only from one that is (`!Open(step) && Open(here)`).
- **Depth.** `RawHeight` draws and collides the coast's shelf below sea level `SeaDeepen` (12) times deeper, in the last 70 rows only; generation, navigation and world hashes read `PolarFracturedWorld.Height` and are unchanged. Bay floors lie at sea level - 12; lead floors at least 10 m under their water (`BuildGapWalls`).
- **Also.** The streamer clamps its window row as `UpdateWindow` does (past the map end the window was rebuilt every frame).
- **Route flags.** (600 m since 0.6.47-test.) `PolarLandforms.Flags` puts a flag every 60 m along each district walk, counted from its start, none within 25 m of a hut's centre or 20 m of another flag; cached on the sector (`flags`, not serialized). The streamer places those in each near chunk (`BuildObjects`, v12+ worlds, off thin ice), a 2.3 m pole and red pennant in one mesh, downwind, no collider. The district chart marks them as red dots.
- **Checked.** Copy of the user's save: 4.5 m in 3 s toward land and out to sea, swimming. Explorers never enter the sea or leads (their grids are unchanged).

## World v17: frozen rivers, October 17-18, 2026 (0.6.24-test)

Plan: [world v17](../plans/polar-rivers-v17.md), built on Codex's terrain handoff and concepts. New expeditions use world v17: v16's scale, huts and walks with frozen rivers cut into the land. v10-v16 worlds generate exactly as before (twenty v16 seeds keep their hashes, `Logs/PolarCompactWorld/v16-identity-v17code`); the user chose to keep the existing coastal chasms and leads.
- **Data** (`PolarRivers.cs`). A river reach is a centreline every 10 m in projected metres with, at each point, the floor's half-width, its absolute height, the bank slope, the depth below the land and the reach (where the banks meet the land). Reaches and their thin-ice windows live on `world.shape.rivers` (a 256 m index), which the explorers' navigation view and the checker's copies share. `CurrentVersion` 17, `RecipeFor(17)` 7, version caps 17; the river signature joins the world hash; the world file keeps format 2 and Continue rebuilds the rivers from the seed.
- **Planning** (`PolarRivers.Plan`, in `GenerateCompact` after the walks and before the ponds; districts in parallel, seeded per district, on the land without knolls). Each district aims at 0-2 medium rivers. A trace starts in open land (`Clearance` open 0.9, 26 cells and 30 m from the seams, outside the coast fade, 80 m from gaps, slope at most 0.25) and steps 20 m downhill with momentum and a meander (no radial term). It stops near huts (110 m beyond the river's edge, the birth hut 170 m), doors (90 m), passes (160 m), other rivers, interior leads (30 m short, so lead water and climb-outs are unchanged), basins, district margins and its length (600-2,000 m); at a coastal chasm it runs on to the bank (a hanging ice front). At a walk it may cross at 50 degrees or more (at most 3 times): the river stops and goes on beyond the walk, which keeps its ground. Medium rivers shorter than 400 m in all are dropped. Tributaries (1-3 per river and up to 2 alone) are traced toward their river or downhill, never cross a walk, and end on the river's floor or in snow.
- **Profiles** (`Build`). Smoothed (Chaikin) and resampled every 10 m. Half-width 10-30 m (sometimes 30-50), with noise over 220 m and 45 m, tapering at ramp ends; depth 4-10 m; bank slope blended between gentle (0.32-0.40) and steep (1.3-2.4) by a 280 m noise, gentle near ramp ends, and a steep run longer than 300 m gets a 70 m gentle stretch in its middle. The floor is the land less the depth, smoothed and never rising downstream (at most 16 m deep, never below -3 m); ramp ends rise back to the land. ## Loading time: October 16, 2026 (0.6.15-test)

## Thin ice

Personal regions have 6-8 snow-covered water areas; station/migration regions have none. Candidates preserve stable surrounding terrain. Ice cells are about 4-6 m with seeded strength and stable routes to declared shores. Actual actor load creates stress; fracture persists. Leaving reduces stress without repair.

`baseTraversal` describes terrain; conservative safe navigation avoids ice windows. Ordinary maps do not reveal hidden strength. Near/distant terrain use the same physical convex cuts. Near ice has support/water; distant ice has no collision and rebuilds from fracture state. Water IDs avoid duplication across chunks. Entry waits for required local support.
