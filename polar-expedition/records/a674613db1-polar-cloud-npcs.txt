# Polar cloud NPCs

## Urgent notices that come while a decision is under way, November 12, 2026 (0.6.144-test)

The question 0.6.141-test left open: should an explorer whose request was in flight at night fall be told again? Yes ([results](../logs/evidence/2026-11-12/dawn-wrap-notices/results.md)).

- **Before.** When a reply came, `Apply` removed every notice that had come while it was decided. Night fall, hunger, cold, a fall, a wound or a failed step in that time was not in the decision's input, so the explorer acted on a plan made without it until the idle notice, up to 60 real seconds (about 1.6 game hours) after the request started.
- **The rule.** A notice keeps its latest urgent reason that is not words (`Notice.alert`), even when words are merged into it. A reply answers the waiting notice as before, unless it holds an alert. Then the explorer is asked again at once. When the reply answered words and the notice also holds words, the explorer is asked about the alert alone, on the planning lane, so words are not answered twice. A notice the reply did not answer because words overtook it (no words answered, words waiting) stays whole, as before.
- **Cost.** One more request for each explorer whose request was in flight when such a notice came: at night fall at most the requests in flight, 3 planning and 10 conversation slots.
- **Checked** (`Tools/CloudNpcCheck`, 211): night fall during a decision asks again with "Night has fallen."; a plain notice during a decision is answered by it; night fall merged with words while the explorer answers words asks again about night fall alone, on the planning lane. The director before the change fails the first check, and the change's first version (keeping only urgent notices that are not social) fails the third.

## Route searches keep their grids, November 12, 2026 (0.6.143-test)

The user's "Yes, fix it here" on the route-search buffers ([results](../logs/evidence/2026-11-12/route-search-grids/results.md)). Routes, their straightening and when explorers search are unchanged.

- **`PolarPathfinder.FindLocal`** takes a set of grids (`Grids`: path costs, parents, the closed set and the search's heap) from a stack of spare sets under a lock, or makes one when none is spare. It clears the set, searches as before, and gives the set back in a `finally`. A set is never used by two searches at once.
- **Why.** Each call allocated the three 512 by 512 arrays anew, 2.3 MB on the worker thread of an explorer's route search (`PolarNpcSimulation`, `Task.Run`). In a walk day that was 127 MB of the 469 MB the game allocated, and it brought Mono's garbage collections sooner, on the main thread or on a worker.
- **Kept for the session:** as many sets as searches ever ran at once. When every explorer searches at a new game or a Continue, about 19 sets, 43 MB. The player's selected route, the suite's checks and the benchmark use the same sets.
- **Measured.** 898 walks on seed 777 give the same routes cell for cell, one after another and on 16 threads at once; a search allocates 22 KB where it allocated 2,603 KB. In a free walk day the workers allocate 5.1 MB where they allocated 127.2 MB over the profiled stretches, and collections fall from 34 to 21, none of them on a worker.

## One request start a frame, November 12, 2026 (0.6.141-test)

The user's "Yes, fix it here" on spreading the explorers' night decisions ([results](../logs/evidence/2026-11-12/night-fall-frames/results.md); the shelter part is in [gameplay](polar-gameplay.md)). What explorers are asked, in what order, and what they decide is unchanged.

- **`PolarNpcDirector.Pump`** starts at most one request a call: the first eligible notice in its usual order (urgent first, then the oldest, then by id). The next frame starts the next one. Pump runs once a frame, so a bunch of ten starts over ten frames, about 0.17 s at 60 fps. These are as they were:
  - the lanes: 10 planning and 3 conversation requests in flight, 60 a minute each;
  - the turn-taking rule;
  - retries and pauses;
  - the 60 s question.
- **Why.** As night falls every awake explorer is told "Night has fallen." in the same rules step, and the next `Pump` started ten requests in one frame. It built the snapshots, prompts and first payloads on the main thread: 7.4 ms of its own time and 1.15 MB allocated, a frame of about 19 ms without the profiler's call stacks. The same happened at a new game, at Continue and at 06:00. Without a key it happened once a minute, with ten requests failing together.
- **Known and left.** An offer or promise change that arrives in the few frames right after such a bunch waits behind the planning starts, at most about ten frames. Before, it started at once on its own lane.
- **Checks.**
  - `Tools/CloudNpcCheck` now checks that one decision starts a frame, the first in order: after one `Pump` the first explorer is busy and the second is not. The old director fails this check.
  - The paid scene bench (`--scenes`) pumps once per cast member before clearing the queue, so its scenes open as before.

## The request ledger's file is written by a worker thread, November 11, 2026 (0.6.136-test)

The user's "Fix it" after the live profile (the section below; [results](../logs/evidence/2026-11-11/ledger-write/results.md)). What the ledger records is unchanged; no rule, save or drawn pixel changes.

- **`NpcApiBudget.Persist`** (called under the ledger's lock by `Reserve` and `Settle`) only notes that the file is behind and, if no write is running, starts one on a worker thread (`Write`). The worker serializes the charges under the lock, then writes the temporary file and moves it over `hourly-ledger.json` outside the lock; a change made during a write is written straight after it. One write runs at a time.
- **A write that fails** (the file held by another program) is dropped and the next change writes again; the charges are in memory either way. Before, the exception went into the request.
- **`Flush()`** waits until no write is running. `ArkNpcClient.Dispose` calls it, so leaving an expedition or quitting leaves every charge in the file; a tool that reads the file while a client is open calls `Flush` first.
- **Measured** on the packages' own compiled code under Unity's Mono, a ledger of 480 charges: `Reserve`, the call a request's start makes on the main thread, takes a median of 0.026 ms where it took 26.0 ms (longest 0.08 ms, was 51.8 ms), and the file holds every charge at the end as before.
- **Verified.** Explorer checks 208 (two new ones read the file back after a flush and after a client is closed), shared rules 2748. The full suite passes on `lw-01`: gameplay 390, physical 3959, physical-resume 18, continue 36, performance 79, buildings 395, fractured 31, handbook 353, weather 84, weather-resume 10, ui 463, handbook-revision 619 (`Logs/PolarV7/0.6.136-test/lw-01`), and so do front page 173, startup 4 and screen-args 3 (`Logs/PolarFront/0.6.136-test/lw-01`).
- **Not measured.** A live day on this package (API calls): the slow frames themselves were not counted again. They were counted on 0.6.137-test, which carries this change (the section above).

## A live day's slow frames: the request ledger's write, November 11, 2026 (profiled, no fix yet)

A profiler run of a live day ([results](../logs/evidence/2026-11-11/live-slow-frames/results.md)), at the user's word after the save cadence change left the slow frames where they were. Nothing here is changed.

- **Finding.** 74 of 85 frames over 25 ms (four minutes of daylight at 60 frames a second) spend a median of 25 ms in `PolarNpcDirector.Pump`, against 0.07 ms in other frames. `Start` calls `ArkNpcClient.CompleteAsync`, which runs on the main thread up to its first wait; with a free slot it goes straight on to `NpcApiBudget.Reserve`, whose `Persist` serializes the last hour's charges and writes and replaces `PolarNpcApi/hourly-ledger.json`. Timed alone under Unity's Mono, that write takes a median of 23 ms with an hour's 480 charges and 19 ms with ten: it is the file's write and replace, not its size.
- **How often.** At every request's start, about one every two seconds in a live day. The second write of a request, when the reply is charged, runs on a worker thread.
- **Also seen.** `PolarNpcDirector.Current()` allocates 10.8 KB in every frame (it reads each explorer's situation every frame, about 39 MB a minute). The explorers' step is the largest marker in 7 of the 85 slow frames; `PolarMvpController.PrepareDoorPassage` allocates up to 240 KB in a frame there.
- **Markers** now round this work: `Polar.Npc.Pump`, `Polar.Simulation.Advance`, `Polar.Npc.Step`, `Polar.Npc.Senses`, `Polar.Npc.Views`, `Polar.Autosave` (`PolarMvpController`, `PolarNpcSimulation`; the director and the client are engine-free and carry none).
- **Fixed** in 0.6.136-test (the section above): the ledger's file is written by a worker thread.

## Explorers' survival fixes and whole-game reruns: November 1, 2026

At the user's request after `wg-live-03` (below), on DeepSeek, the causes of death that were the explorers' or the engine's fault, with least change ([gameplay](polar-gameplay.md) top section; [design decisions](../../design-decisions.md#tents-stand-clear-of-huts-november-1-2026)).
- **Hours on foot** (`PolarNpcManual.Ground` .5, was .85). In `wg-live-03` walks took about twice the view's hours (about 1.9 m/s where the view said 4.25). `WalkHours` now also takes the person's own pace (`PolarItemDefinition.Speed`: load, wounds, cold, fatigue) for places, Pole Station, the rescue point and people last seen; the manual says 1 km takes about 10.7 hours when fresh.
- **Manual.**
  - A hut's stores last days: move on by day while still fed, warm and rested.
  - A box seen is a dated record: search it again only when someone else has been there. Explorers had searched the same boxes over and over.
  - A walk of days is a chain of walks.
  - Ruins may still hold stores, and Pole Station is stocked beyond any hut. This is the GDD's supply gradient: on seed 777 ruins held 30-80 food units, Pole Station 228, and most home huts none by day 4.
  - Tents stand on open ground, never in or against a hut; strike yours and carry it when you move on.
- **Refused replies explained** (`PolarNpcDirector.Failed`). A refused reply is asked again with the reason ("Your last reply was refused: ..."). Before, the same question came back unexplained and DeepSeek repeated what was refused: 157 unknown commands and about 200 unknown references in `wg-live-03`. A first command that cannot come first now names the commands that can; an unknown reference says every id must be one the view lists.
- **Tents clear of huts** (`PolarMvpController.ResolveGroundDrop`, for everyone). No tent stands within a hut's walls and a tent's radius. Jones pitched inside an unloaded hut, whose only floor is its anchor; the tent's box stood over the crates, and Jones died beside two tins after 273 failed takes. An explorer's failed pitch near a hut says so: "A tent cannot stand in or against a hut: go out of its door and a few steps away, then pitch".
- **Tents carried on** (`PolarNpcSimulation.PackTent`). A `travel`, `explore` or `rescue` walk first strikes the explorer's own tent, once per pitch, when it stands within 15 m, fits in the pack and nobody else is in it. Finished steps make room in the four-command plan, else its last step goes (`PolarSaveService` refuses longer plans; the rerun's autosaves failed until that was handled). In `wg-live-05`, 8 of the 10 dead had left their tent standing 270-1,550 m behind them; in `wg-live-06` the dead lay beside theirs.
- **Reflexes.**
  - **Swimming.** An explorer in the water without a `swim` command swims out by reflex the way it faced, and its plan goes on from the edge (`Swim`). Two drowned in `wg-live-05`: in water only `swim` may come first, and their replies kept naming `travel`.
  - **Eating.** `eat` takes any food that can be eaten there when the named id is not food in the pack, or is a frozen ration away from a fire. The validator no longer checks eat's id. Shaw starved naming tins just picked up from the floor; 33 eats failed "That is not food" in six days, and 86 named frozen rations with tins in the pack.
  - **Warmth.** The warmth reflex shelters only in a known hut with a usable stove (a ruin has none); otherwise it pitches the tent.
- **View.** Past the comfortable weight, a warning gives the load and the maximum. Vale's takes failed as too heavy 19 times in one day with no weight shown.
- **CloudNpcCheck** builds again: its project lacked `PolarWalkEffort.cs` and `PolarRescue.cs`, missing since 0.6.87-test.

## NPC agency: October 18, 2026 (0.6.25-test)

Every high-level choice an explorer makes now comes from the model, from that explorer's own knowledge; the engine carries it out with the shared actions and reports the actual result ([plan](../plans/polar-npc-agency.md), [decisions](../../design-decisions.md#npc-agency-october-18-2026-planned-0625-test)). The sections below this one describe the rule-era system and are historical where they conflict.

- **Contract** (`PolarNpcMind`, `NpcModelPrompts`). A reply is `{commands: 1-4 of {kind, target, item, n}, say, quotes[, terms]}`. `NpcKnowledgeSnapshot` holds the explorer's body, own items (`#id`), the 8 nearest known places, people in view (four described, the rest as `alsoInView`), people out of sight where last seen, the hut's stove (lit or out, and its oil) and its boxes as last seen (grouped by kind), pitched tents within 30 m, offers involving it with exact terms (`PolarLabels.OfferTerms`), its plan and last result, memory, calls heard, and the trigger. `can` lists the command kinds possible now on feasibility alone (owning a rifle, being in water, having a pending offer); the instructions list them with their parameters (`NpcModelPrompts.Help`). `Validate` accepts only those kinds and only ids from the snapshot; physical feasibility is left to the executing action. A loosely named thing resolves from the explorer's own knowledge instead of failing the decision: a call to someone unknown goes to anyone, `stove`/`shelter` mean the hut here, `take`/`pour`/`place` use the box the item was seen in, and a tent command uses the one tent here the explorer may use. `terms` is one text ("give #id xN; ask Food tin xN; record #place; askRecords N; tent #id; promise"), parsed by `ParseTerms`. A "say" command is treated as speech only, invalid speech is dropped, and only exact heard words are kept as quotes.
- **Prompt size.** Each place, person, memory and line of speech is one short line (`Lines` converter). `Fit` keeps the body, `can`, the trigger, offers and own items, and drops older claims, beliefs, dialogue and events first, then speech habits, people beyond the first, places beyond the nearest, the plan's later steps and box contents, the latest words last. Heaviest case: 3,867 of 3,900 bytes. Player messages are limited to 300 bytes.
- **Pipeline** (`PolarNpcDirector`). `Notify(id, reason, urgent, social, partner, heard)` is the only way to ask: the simulation reports first sight or reacquisition after 30 game s, wounds, waking, body thresholds (hungry, cold, exhausted, nightfall, badly hurt) and command results; `Pump` itself notices new beliefs, events and offer or promise-proposal changes, and asks every explorer again after a quiet real minute. Urgent first, then oldest; one request per explorer; 2 planning and 1 interaction slot with 60 starts a minute each, 8 s deadlines; sleepers wait. A decision replaces the plan (`continue` keeps it), speaks its words through `PolarNpcMind.Dialogue` and answers everything queued before it. Only the decision id makes a reply stale.
- **Failures.** Nothing falls back to rules. The plan continues while it can; otherwise the explorer waits. A transient failure retries the same question after 2, 4, 8, 16, then 30 s; a missing key, HTTP 401/403 or a spent budget pauses all requests for a minute. `mind.status` keeps the reason for diagnostics only.
- **Player words** (`Talk`). The topic gate (`NpcModelPrompts.Topic`: the new words as data, the last four lines between the two, fixed rules with short examples) classifies first; nothing answers the words before it. `unrelated` → `MakeHostile`; otherwise the words are spoken and one decision on the player lane (15 s for both requests) answers them and its commands run. The player's consent is still a button. If the gate fails, the words are not delivered and the panel says "No answer".
- **Execution** (`PolarNpcSimulation.Execute`). Deleted: `PolarNpcCalls`, `EatAvailable`, `RestAt`, `Camp`, `CaughtOut`, `TentAnswer`, `VisitSupplies`, `SeekWindShelter`, `TurnBack`, `CanReachInDaylight`, `PressOn`, the gunshot retreat, `ChooseRoute`'s own destination, `Respond`, the 35 m pursuit reset, automatic stowing, `RuleChoice`, `OfferSafe`. Each command reuses the old mechanics: travel and explore through the shared route search (a busy queue waits for the same place), approach and leave by straight walks (a leave goes away from the person, or from a hut within 30 m, on a straight way clear all along), shelter/exit by the hut walk path and tent entrance, investigate/take/place/pour at the box's front, stove and burner within reach, pitch at the 8 spots ahead, sleep at a cot or bag, aim, shoot (`TryShot`, n shots), stab and throw, offers and the rest through `PolarActions`. A command that cannot be done ends the plan with the action's own reason, which asks for the next decision.
- **Harpoons.** `PolarMvpController.ResolveStrike`/`HitActor`: one segment-against-capsule test for stabs and thrown flights, for the player and explorers alike, with `ClearLine` (physics near the player, terrain and hut boxes far off) between; damage lands once.
- **Hostility.** `mind.hostileToPlayer`, `hostileAt`, `hostileSource`. The transition is idempotent: it cancels the explorer's request, removes waiting offers and promise proposals with the player (an active promise stays; harm breaches it), speaks "You've lost your senses. I won't talk to you." once, and only the addressee keeps the request's words. While it recognises the player in sight it attacks with its held weapon, else a rifle with rounds, else a harpoon close in (`ForcedAttack`); out of sight it goes once to where it last saw them. A model's own attack on the player takes precedence; its cooperation with the player is dropped.
- **Saves.** NpcMind gains `agency`, `agenda`, `step`, `progress`, `result` and the hostility fields; `plan` and `foodTarget` are gone. Reading a pre-agency v7 save copies the file byte for byte to `…pre-agency-<first 12 of its SHA-256>.json` (once per content), then sets empty plans; legacy requested offers become one ask. `Validate` checks plan length, step and command kinds.
- **Checks.** CloudNpcCheck (rewritten around a scripted test model): 143 core, 3 information-boundary, 4 event, 2 call, 41 prompt-bound and 2 deadline checks. Editor rehearsal (`PolarNpcAgencyRehearsal`): 13/13. Live provider (`--live-agency`, two runs): 19/20 heaviest decisions valid; the gate caught 16/16 unrelated requests and judged none of 21 game-related messages unrelated.
- **Live days** (`PolarNpcDayDiagnostic`, seed 777, twenty explorers, one game day of 15 real minutes each, `Logs/NpcDayDiagnostic/agency-live-01` to `03`). Every day all twenty lived, none fell below 32 °C, nobody was hurt and supplies were only consumed. Day 1: 529 decisions, 643 requests, ¥1.81, P95 3.5 s, 114 decisions refused for unknown references. Day 2: ¥1.80, 161 refused (98 loosely named places, boxes, people or tents); 44 failed commands lit a stove without oil. Day 3, with the reference resolution and the stove line: 580 decisions, 669 requests (5 missed the deadline), ¥1.91, P95 3.85 s, 89 refused (mostly `investigate`/`take` naming ids never seen); failed commands rose to 232, mostly stove commands: oil added to a full stove (99; the snapshot does not give the 2-day capacity) and lighting a stove without oil (66). ## Route legs clear unwalkable cells: October 10, 2026

`Move` drops an explorer's route where a half-metre step fails the explorers' step test (`CanMoveTerrain`): it clears the route and sets `destinationSite=-1`. Explorers whose route clipped the corner of an unwalkable navigation cell stopped there, and often searched the same line again. There were two causes.
- **Straightening read the cells every 4 m.** `LineClear` tested a straight leg with `PolarFracturedWorld.Segment`, which reads the cells every 4 m, so a leg could clip a cell between two readings. On seed 777's 478 explorer routes between neighbouring huts, 19 legs entered an unwalkable cell (27 failing steps on 7 routes). Seeds 12345 and 494993 had 14 and 22 legs (29 and 39 steps).
- **Single-precision steps bowed legs off their line.** `Move` stepped on from each rounded position. Positions are floats 4-8 mm apart at the world's coordinates, and the same rounding repeated step after step. On seed 777 a leg bowed a median 12 cm, and up to 86 cm, off the line it was tested on.

**What explorers did.** `PolarRouteCornerCapture` ran in the Editor on the game's own seed-777 world, with the production `Step` at the game's 1 s NPC step and every other explorer asleep. The game's routes failed on 7 of the 478 walks (23 steps). Walked:
- on 6 the explorer stopped at the corner;
- on 4 of those it then looped: it walked back to search a route to the same hut and stopped at the same corner every 21-33 s, never arriving (Irwin, sector 30 hut 9 to hut 5: 29 stops in 600 s);
- on 2 the new route missed the corner;
- on 1 its steps passed over the clipped corner.

**The fix.**
- **`StraightClear`.** Straightening and the hops between a hut's approach point and its route end (`RouteEnd`, `RouteStart`) now test a leg with `Segment` as before plus `PolarFracturedWorld.CellsWalkable`.
  - It reads the leg every half metre and traces every cell between two readings along the line joining them on the grid, so no cell the leg enters is missed.
  - A leg between two cell centres often passes exactly through a cell corner. So the four cells round any corner it passes within 5 cm of, and the cells within 5 cm of its ends, must be walkable too.
- **`Move` steps along the leg's own line** (`Along`): each step ends on the line from where the explorer set out toward its waypoint, so rounding no longer accumulates. Legs now stay within 2 cm of their line (median 4 mm). The leg starts afresh after a load, a new waypoint, or anything that moves the explorer more than 5 cm off it.
- **Each part alone left failures.** In a single-precision emulation of `Move` on the three seeds:
  - straightening alone left 0, 2 and 5 failing steps;
  - stepping alone left 14, 15 and 20.

  In the Editor, straightening alone left Olsen (sector 36, hut 9 to hut 4) stopping once: his walk had drifted 15 cm across a corner his leg clears by 8 cm.
- **A route goes where it was searched for.** The first NPC day run with the two changes above left 3 explorers out at dawn, 1.1-1.3 km from home at 33 C.
  - A travel plan's route search was still running when a visit inside the hut (a supply box, the cot) set `destinationSite` to that hut. `ChooseRoute` then joined the route toward the other hut to that hut's walk path.
  - With the hut it left as its destination, `TurnBack` never turned the explorer back.
  - The race is older. Searches now take about twice as long, which leaves more game steps for it.
  - `ChooseRoute` now restores the destination the search was for (kept with the route's ends).
- **Unchanged:** world generation (world hashes), the grid search, `Move`'s step test, and the boulder field and its detours (`WayRound`, `LegWalkable`). Detour legs are now walked on the lines `LegWalkable` tested.
- **Cost.** Straightening takes a median 80 ms per route on the route search's background thread (was 43 ms; at most 232 ms, was 118 ms). Seed 777's routes keep 24 more waypoints (8,347), with the same total length.

## Calls and noticing cold: October 7, 2026

Two hand-offs are finished at the user's request, which also authorized the edits to NPC-owned files. Explorers now call out, answer calls and react to them (GDD 3.4.3), and they notice when someone else is visibly cold (GDD 5.9). Evidence: [npc-calls-and-cold](../logs/evidence/2026-10-07/npc-calls-and-cold/).

**Calls and reactions.**
- **Engine record.** `PolarActions.Call(speaker,call,addressedTo=-1,answer=false)` still gives every awake listener within call range a "Called out" belief. It now also keeps an unsaved list of the calls each listener heard, with the voice's position, for 120 game seconds (at most 8 each). `ForgetHeardCalls` marks them handled, so frozen acceptance scenes don't answer stale calls.
- **Rules.** The rules are in `PolarNpcCalls`, appended to `PolarNpcSurvival.cs` so both offline tools compile them. Dispositions come from personality weights:
  - wary: Frightened, or a Hoarder with strangers;
  - silent: Hoarder, Ordinary survivor;
  - helpful: Sharer, Schemer, Organizer, Moralist;
  - in need: satiety under 40, below 35.5 °C, hydration under 40, or injury over 25.
- **Answers.** Words then movement, by precedence, for "Who's there?" / "Stay where you are." / "I need help.". "I mean no harm." is never answered, which ends an exchange.

  | Who | Answer | Movement |
  |---|---|---|
  | Danger (an identified promise breaker, or anyone seen aiming) | "Stay where you are." | avoid |
  | Wary | "Stay where you are."; nothing to "Stay" | avoid on "Stay" |
  | In need | "I need help."; nothing to "Help" | hold on "Stay" |
  | Silent | nothing | hold on "Stay" |
  | Helpful | "I mean no harm." | hold on "Stay", approach on "Help" |
  | Others (Pragmatist) | "I mean no harm."; to "Help", "Who's there?" from a stranger, nothing from someone met | hold on "Stay" |

- **Opening calls.** An explorer may call to the nearest person in view within call and sight range, when nobody is within 4 m:
  - "Stay where you are." in danger;
  - "I need help." in need;
  - "Who's there?" to a stranger, or a warning if wary, or nothing if silent.

  A recognised person who is fine gets no call. The same opening is not repeated to the same person until they have been beyond calling range, a game day has passed, or, for "I need help.", the need has ended.
- **Pacing.**
  - Calls are answered within 5 game seconds, at least 4 s after the explorer's own last call.
  - At most 2 answers per pair per 120 s.
  - Openings need 20 s since the explorer's last call and no exchange between the pair for 120 s.
  - Reactions use only calls addressed to anyone (the player's) or to that explorer, so bystanders stay quiet.
- **Identity.** A voice counts as known when the listener has met the speaker, at any range (as heard words are attributed); a face is recognised within recognition range (40 m; 12 m in a blizzard). An unidentified voice is never taken for a known promise breaker.
- **Movement** (`PolarNpcSimulation.Encounter`, after the wind-shelter step):
  - hold faces the voice for up to 15 s and keeps the route;
  - approach walks to the voice, rounded to 10 m and 10 degrees, or to the live person while in view; it stops in talking range with one social request and gives up after 90 s;
  - avoid walks to one probed point 25 m away for up to 30 s.

  After an approach or avoid the explorer walks back to where it left its route when that line is walkable, and otherwise re-plans to the same place. A hold or approach toward someone seen to be dangerous turns into an avoid.
- **Survival first.** No encounter movement:
  - at night, sheltered or at a lit fire;
  - below 35.5 °C, under 40 water or over 75 fatigue;
  - within the defence hour of an attack, or within half a game hour of a gunshot (so the existing retreat runs);
  - with anyone within 4 m;
  - while a route search is pending.

  Travel planning, rest and turning back end any encounter. Words are still allowed at night.
- **Planner.**
  - Planning (not conversation) gains `approach-<call>` and `avoid-<call>` for the latest voice heard, with no speaker id, so the model cannot link a voice to a remembered stranger.
  - The planning view gains a compact `heardCalls` list (the last two within 60 s, named only if met), which Fit never trims.

## Knowledge and decisions

`NpcKnowledgeSnapshot` is a whitelist: own body/inventory, own dated location records, visible appearance and actions, participant events, attributed conversation and feasible candidates. Other actors' private maps never determine whether a share candidate exists; the NPC uses its own sharing history. Visible activity includes actual rifle aiming, rather than hidden intent. The current conversation partner remains in the snapshot even in a crowded encounter. `earlierConversation` confirms a previous reply by this NPC to that person; the current question and overheard words cannot set it. `addressedTo` retains the original addressed listener through retelling, so overheard "we" is not silently reassigned to the receiver. A bounded history is explicitly partial; an omitted detail never proves no prior meeting. Ordinary expression omits incidental other-pair overhearing to avoid reassigning their words to this conversation; planning and explicit retelling retain it. Expression uses a separate compact instruction and physical-language projection: body conditions replace internal scales, gear describes actual chambers and bolts, and experiences use known names/places and game-calendar dates. Personality weights and engine IDs stay in planning. Sole actions never invent a second participant; known place names survive context trimming. Previous words remain attributed speech, never direct event facts. Reviewed schema/quantity/duration narration and inconsistent source-removal modifiers are rejected. Model requests contain no global stock ledger, other personality, remote live coordinates or future random state.

`INpcModelClient` separates provider responses from gameplay. `NpcDecisionProposal` contains a supplied candidate identifier and verbatim current-player quotes. Unknown candidates and unattributed quotes fail validation. Expression is a second English JSON response with the current player statement and the chosen proposal. Speaker/listener zero is serialized explicitly, not as missing attribution; the model is told that every other actor number, including 0, is an ordinary expedition member and is never told which person the human controls. Current engine candidates are trusted facts; spoken claims remain unverified. Outgoing record candidates explicitly state that the NPC sends its own record. Foreground text creates no transfers or consent: the player confirms actions using explicit controls, and the NPC independently decides its side. English validation also rejects interface/JSON debris and meta speech inside utterances. UI rows show only the speaker name and the measured dialogue text.

Background proposals choose known destinations, reserve targets, fire/rest, sharing, promises and withdrawal, surplus gifts, barter, refusal, help, notes, joint clearing and watch. Actual consumption, urgent cold, immersion, attack response, ammunition and movement remain engine-owned. Autonomous transfers preserve a food margin and ammunition; current food never spoils. Barter advertises a desired kind without reading the recipient's backpack; the recipient selects an actual held item before acceptance. `PolarActions` validates stock, capacity, position and bilateral commitment atomically. A waiting trade prevents duplicate offers while leaving promise choices independent. Labels disclose actual terms and relevant consent, without unrelated private inventory.

All gameplay commits run on the world thread. Background network results enter a completion queue; actor death, damage, movement beyond perception, changed candidates, superseded decisions or disposed expeditions invalidate them. Successful damage permanently advances the victim's cloud decision identifier. Clearing the temporary aggressor flag, including during accelerated simulation, cannot revive a pre-hit request. Rejected damage does not advance it; since rule version 5, harm during a promise is not rejected but recorded as a breach. Read/load recreates request lifetime; in-flight network operations are never serialized. Background tasks preserve existing movement and survival while awaiting a response.

## Transport and costs

Ark endpoint: `https://ark.cn-beijing.volces.com/api/v3/responses`; model: `deepseek-v4-1-flash-260910`. Strict JSON Schema, `stream=true`, `store=false`, disabled thinking, no tools and 512 output tokens. SSE deltas are buffered until `response.completed`; incomplete, malformed and failed streams never execute. The implementation accepts the final `[DONE]` marker.

The input bound conservatively counts UTF-8 bytes for system/input/schema, allowing 3900 bytes plus protocol overhead within 4096 tokens. Fitting targets 3600 bytes and removes older or less relevant context while keeping current body, inventory, candidates and execution constraints mandatory. The snapshot admits four received lines, two previous own lines, four salient beliefs and eight relevant events. UI source labels are excluded. Expression keeps only its selected candidate; oversized mandatory input fails explicitly. Full-memory tests exercise all twenty identities and their current choices.

Transfers, record sharing, promise acceptance/withdrawal, refusal, help and watch have an opening sentence bound to the selected action. A concise personal explanation cannot substitute goods, direction, quantities or duration. Speech targets a short reply below 280 characters within the validator's 400-character bound. Its single repair reiterates brevity and the exact opening without referring to an absent procedural outcome. Instructions avoid sentence-count and procedural outcome annotations, which were echoed in earlier model runs. Expression drops extra atlas places and bystanders before recent addressed conversation. Received accounts identify how words arrived and remain unverified; duplicate addressed words already present in the conversation are omitted, while unique addressed reports are retained. JSON braces, backticks, automatic annotation tags and observed instruction echoes are rejected. The live harness preserves raw decision/expression results, including repair attempts and failed turns. Free explanations can still be imperfect. The canonical proposal remains in diagnostic source data, hidden in normal UI; only validated choices can change the world.

There are three lanes. Player conversation has one reserved slot and a fifteen-second total deadline, including queueing, both stages and at most one repair. Planning has two slots, eight-second deadlines and twelve actual starts per real minute. NPC conversation has one request slot, one pair at a time, at most six pairs/24 actual requests per real minute and a fifteen-second pair deadline. Each actor waits at least thirty real seconds; each pair waits 120. Pending offers and promises prioritize a response. Oldest queued planning work runs first; duplicate work merges. Participant events, newly received physical information, completed goals and 120-second reviews trigger reconsideration. Duplicate information does not advance the actor's information revision. Sleep acceleration increases neither requests nor budget. Player conversation preempts a conflicting pair and expires its old response. Healthy speakers pause travel during both player and NPC conversation; urgent survival and defence retain authority.

The key is read only from `ARK_API_KEY`. It is never saved, printed or included in a package. `Tools/launch_cloud_npcs.ps1` can collect a masked key and pass it in the child environment, restoring the original environment afterward. No local model is started by this system.

`NpcApiBudget` reserves 0.012288 yuan before each request (4096 input at 2 yuan/million plus 512 output at 8 yuan/million), settles known usage and keeps the maximum charge for unmetered failures. Reservations and charges persist atomically under the application's `PolarNpcApi/hourly-ledger.json`; reload/restart cannot reset the allowance. A corrupt ledger conservatively consumes the hour's allowance. This is a conservative local estimate, not a provider billing guarantee; current provider prices and billing remain external. Rolling cost and input/output token totals appear only in the opt-in developer overlay (`-npcDiagnostics`, `POLAR_NPC_DIAGNOSTICS=1` or `launch_cloud_npcs.ps1 -Diagnostics`), never in normal play.

Failures use the same archetype weights and speak an in-character line for the chosen rule candidate (`PolarNpcMind.RuleLine`). The rule mode and failure reason remain in `NpcMind.status` and the stored dialogue source for diagnostics and the developer overlay. Transport failures do not create model-generated text, free resources, protection or exemptions.

## Rule version 5 and physical memory (October 3, 2026)

`PolarNpcSurvival` eats tins anywhere and frozen rations at a lit fire, seeks fire hydration and warmth, relights retained stove fuel and uses finite carried fuel. v5 NPCs no longer depend on bottles, snow melting, timed preparation or spoilage. Terrain, stock, weights and travel scale remain authoritative.

`NpcBelief` saves at most 96 observations and sourced reports: origin, original source, latest speaker, subject/pair, event and reception times, hops and channel. Retelling preserves origin, original named source and event time while recording the latest messenger and increasing hops. A speaker's firsthand observation becomes an attributed report for the receiver; it cannot retain the label "My own observation." Reports and notes never become direct observations. Speakers can conceal supplies or mislead; they cannot alter factual records. Previous own words constrain later explanations. Salient deaths, breaches and transfers survive ordinary memory eviction. There is no global reputation or news feed.

Face-to-face speech and overhearing require physical hearing range and an awake listener. Promise acceptance and withdrawal reach actual listeners; harm reaches actual sight witnesses. Seeing harm without knowledge of the private agreement records a conflict, without revealing a hidden promise. The older roster experience summary uses that same actual observation, rather than generating its own breach witness list. Transfer and hit observations require sight of both participants; seeing only the giver or shooter cannot reveal an unseen recipient or result. Identities remain unknown until contact. Late body discovery requires actual local sight and occlusion checks; it records discovery time without hidden inventory, cause or exact death time. Sleeping people do not learn ordinary talk or visual events. The production NPC step skips exterior discovery and automatic identity contact while asleep. The shared v5 contact action checks each recipient's wakefulness: an awake observer can recognize a sleeper, while the sleeper receives no identity record. NPC observation of a sleeping player cannot advance the player's encounter act. Controlled packaged-Player states check the production step and the shared contact entrance. Historical rules are preserved.

Signed notes are read only through actual hut search. NPC writing uses their own history and knowledge. Joint clearing records actual contributors for both people. The player's roster has a full bounded field journal of their own actions, sightings and physically received words with provenance. The pre-existing expedition roster contains every name and portrait, as specified by GDD 2.1. Encounter recognition links a perceived person to a roster entry; unencountered entries disclose no live situation, stock or intentions.

Watch keeps an NPC awake while companions sleep, subject to exhaustion; actually perceived aiming lets the watcher warn sleepers within hearing. Gunshots wake hearing sleepers, including promise partners. An asleep actor cannot read a hidden defensive intent.

`PolarNpcCombat` raises a held rifle for 1.5 real seconds before firing; sleep acceleration cannot shorten the warning. Accuracy depends on distance, movement, weather, cold and injury and is never certain. Every shot uses ammunition; the shot sequence persists to prevent rerolling the same saved shot. Defence has bounded pursuit and can flee.

Autonomous confrontation currently requires a recently observed resource competitor and desperation and excludes an active promise. Autonomous promise-breaking remains disabled pending the GDD's betrayal calibration. The shared v5 engine permits harm during a promise and records a breach. This is **not** the withdrawal-only fallback, which would need a real engine protection gate. Long-term betrayal frequency and emotional playtesting remain open.
